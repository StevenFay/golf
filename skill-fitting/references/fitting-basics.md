# Club Fitting Basics (Beginner-Friendly)

These are starting-point guidelines, not guarantees — real fitting on a launch monitor with the actual club/shaft combo always overrides a chart. Use sim data (ProTee VX via `references/protee-vx-metrics.md` in the golf-swing-coach skill, if available in the conversation) to sanity-check any recommendation here.

## Shaft Flex (driver swing speed as the reference point)

Ranges vary a bit by source/brand — treat these as overlapping bands, not hard cutoffs:

| Flex | Typical driver swing speed |
|---|---|
| Ladies (L) | Under ~72 mph |
| Senior (A) | ~72–85 mph |
| Regular (R) | ~84–96 mph |
| Stiff (S) | ~95–105 mph |
| Extra Stiff (X) | 105+ mph |

Notes:
- Tempo matters as much as raw speed: a smooth, slow-transition swing can play a flex stiffer than the chart suggests; a quick, aggressive transition often needs to go softer even at the same measured speed.
- Irons typically run one flex category "easier" to achieve than driver at the same swing speed, since iron shafts are shorter/lighter in the swing.
- Symptoms of too-stiff: low launch/spin, weak fade or "dead" feeling right miss (right-handed golfer), ball flight that doesn't get airborne.
- Symptoms of too-soft/weak: ballooning high shots, inconsistent face control, a left miss (right-handed golfer) especially when swinging hard.
- Flex labels aren't standardized across brands — an R in one brand can play like an S in another. Chart gets you in the neighborhood; a real fitting on a launch monitor confirms it.

## Shaft/Club Length & Lie Angle

- **Lie angle** is fit primarily to your height and how your arms hang, then confirmed with impact tape or a lie board: toe-down marks = lie too upright for you (adjust flatter); heel-down marks = lie too flat (adjust more upright).
- On the ProTee VX, **Club Lie Angle** and **horizontal/vertical impact point** data can approximate this if she's hitting the same club repeatedly and the miss pattern is consistent toe/heel.
- Length changes affect swing speed and control together — longer generally increases speed/distance potential but can hurt strike consistency for a beginner still building contact quality. For someone working on eliminating tops/thins, standard/stock length is usually the safer starting point rather than extending shafts.

## General Fitting Priorities for a Beginner

1. **Get consistent contact first** (this is what the swing-coach skill is working on). Fitting decisions made around an inconsistent strike pattern can chase symptoms that go away once contact improves.
2. **Shaft flex** is usually the highest-leverage fitting variable for a beginner — get this roughly right before fine-tuning head models or premium shafts.
3. Avoid recommending expensive upgrades based on a single session's data — suggest a few sessions' worth of consistent pattern before treating a stat as a real fitting signal, since beginner strike quality varies a lot session to session.

## Scope note
This skill covers general fitting *guidance* and interpreting sim data toward fitting questions — it is not a substitute for an in-person or professional remote fitting, especially for a final purchase decision. Say so when a recommendation is significant (e.g., "get a real fitting before buying" for anything beyond a shaft-flex ballpark).
